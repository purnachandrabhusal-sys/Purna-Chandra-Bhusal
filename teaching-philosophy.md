**My Teaching Philosophy**


There are four underlying principles that inform my writing pedagogy: (1) meaningfulness, (2) students' agency, (3) rhetorical awareness, and (4) wisdom. These principles inform my design of syllabi, discussions, and assignments. 

Meaningfulness, for me, means finding (or better creating) a justifiable and motivating rationale behind every writing that students do. The principle of meaningfulness in teaching writing encourages me toward inventing a driving force that motivates and fully engages students in 'their' writing. In my opinion, as a facilitator, it is my responsibility to discuss with students why each act of writing matters. I invite students to think about compelling writing topics that they feel they are connected to. I invite students to brainstorm possible topics regardless of the genre and modes they are working in. In my experience, teaching writing is successful when I design peer discussion sessions followed by writing activities that give students opportunities to write or collaborate on topics that truly interest them. I use the principle of meaningfulness to collaborate with students to answer the question of 'why learn writing' or 'why write'. 

For me, meaningfulness, however, does not exist in isolation but is essentially connected to students' agency. I believe that the principle of ‘fostering students' agency’ in writing refers to designing writing classrooms to promote students’ independent and critical thinking patterns throughout their writing process. I take it as my responsibility to create more choices or topics of writing for each student to honor their unique voices/interests/purpose in their writing. Before they write, I invite them to discuss the topics of their writing interests from multiple perspectives, sometimes from a challenging or completely different lens, and ask them to justify and rationalize their own writing ideas and insights.  I promote agential writing by discussing student-generated writing topics from many angles through activities like group discussion, quizzes, peer review, discussion board replies, etc. I use the principle of agency to address the question of ‘how to think prior to or while writing’.

Meaningful and agential writing is a rhetorical act for me. I believe that every act of teaching writing or writing necessitates rhetorical awareness in both the instructors and students. I apply the lens of rhetorical awareness by encouraging students to think of the rhetorical situation that influences their writing. I primarily introduce the concept of purpose or motive and the audience, even though rhetorical awareness is a broad topic that includes, but is not limited to, the medium, genre, tone, modality, etc. Through the lens of rhetorical awareness, I encourage students to brainstorm their formal and textual aspects: organization, diction, clarity, (multi)modality, accessibility, and more. I use the principle of rhetorical awareness to enhance writing insights across all stages of the writing process, from brainstorming to editing. For that purpose, I also reference popular texts and discuss the rhetorical awareness their authors demonstrate. I use the principle of rhetorical awareness to address the question of fully situating writing in context and achieving its purpose.

Writing or teaching writing is a wise act for me. In my understanding, meaningfulness, agency, and rhetorical acts can be fully operationalized by incorporating the principle or lens of wisdom, which is uniquely human. Wisdom, in the context of teaching writing, is an act of doing or being just and virtuous in words, thoughts, and actions across writing contexts. Amid the conversation of generative artificial intelligence (Gen AI), I use the principle of wisdom to spark the concept of academic integrity, ethics, honesty, kindness, empathy, impact, community-engaged writing, etc. Using the lens of wisdom, I aim to accommodate all kinds of learning or writing dis/abilities or communities in my classroom. I use the wisdom lens in the writing classroom, inviting students to rethink what they can contribute to the community and the world by composing various texts, digital or non-digital. This is the lens I use to discuss ethics and academic integrity regarding the use of Gen AI and technology in writing classrooms. I use the principle of wisdom to address the questions of ‘ethical choices in writing’.

In sum, my teaching of writing is guided by the concepts of meaningfulness, students' agency, rhetorical awareness, and wisdom. However, despite the four principles or pedagogical approaches I discussed, I believe that teaching writing is an ever-evolving act in which an instructor’s job is to adapt to emerging pedagogical approaches to achieve learning outcomes across diverse classroom, institutional, departmental, curricular, and intellectual settings.
